Where this is going

Nine lessons in two tracks, feeding the exercises in this repo. Two are written. The rest are a plan, not a contract.

Updated 26 Oct 2026 · 2 of 9 complete

Each lesson is one idea, 10–15 minutes, with a Part 1 that stands on its own. The ordering below is roughly the order of dependence: the structured-output track makes a small model's output safe to use in code, and the operations track makes it fast and predictable enough to ship. They interleave on purpose — switching tracks is a form of spacing, and it helps things stick.

keep_alive and warm-up, streaming so the wait is felt less, what happens under concurrent requests, and the failure modes of leaving a model running for days.

How firm is this?

Lessons 03 and 04 are solid. They answer questions the first two lessons raised, and one of them fixes a real bug in exercise 01.

05 through 09 are a sketch. Teaching adapts to what you turn out to already know, what you find interesting, and what breaks when you build. Some will merge, some will split, and at least one will be replaced by something neither of us has thought of yet. If a lesson here stops looking worth it, it comes off the list — a plan you follow past its usefulness is worse than no plan.

The honest estimate: nine lessons, of which two are done, at your pace of one or two per sitting. The count will move. The two tracks will not.

Want a different shape — more depth on one track, fewer lessons, or a jump straight to an exercise? Say so. The map is a tool for you to steer with, not a syllabus you owe anyone.
